Strategic Rivalries Through the Lens of Game Theory

An Interactive Analysis of Five Real-World Competitive Rivalries

About This Project

Game Theory studies how rational players make decisions when their outcomes depend on the actions of others. Businesses, governments and organizations constantly compete by anticipating the strategies of rivals. This project analyzes five real-world rivalries using strategic game theory concepts such as players, strategy sets, payoff matrices, game trees, dominant strategies and Nash equilibrium.

Conclusion

This project demonstrates how Game Theory explains real-world strategic decision-making. Across five different rivalries, firms and organizations continuously adapt their strategies based on competitors' actions, leading to outcomes such as Nash Equilibrium, cooperation, or competition. These examples show that strategic thinking is essential in business, public policy, and competitive markets.
